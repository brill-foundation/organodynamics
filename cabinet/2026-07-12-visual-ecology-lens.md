# Visual Ecology — a lens for stewardship

## 1. The frame

The Laboratory is an **environment**, not a user interface. The steward's
distinction: *consistency makes everything similar; ecology makes everything
belong.* Each visual object should occupy a distinct **ecological niche** and
coexist without competing — a museum label, a handwritten note, a
constitutional document, a historical record, and a room sign are different
**species** sharing one habitat, not variations of one component.

This is the visual counterpart of a principle already in the Constitution:
each object owns a distinct **responsibility** (charter §2), and the furniture
is deliberately unintelligent (§5). Ecology extends that from *what an object
does* to *what perceptual role it plays.*

## 2. Status — a lens, not a law

This is an **emerging lens, not ratified architecture.** It drives no change.
Per the CAB-005 limit, a concept is not encoded into architecture before it is
necessary; this is a way of seeing, held open and revisable by evidence. Whether
visual ecology becomes a stated principle of the place is itself left open —
reality has not voted.

## 3. The niches (the steward's taxonomy)

- **Architecture** — structural masses that define the building: the rooms
  grid, the page frame, the reading-room document card, the observatory window.
- **Wayfinding** — orients and directs: nav, the "← THE DOOR" return, room
  titles, the uppercase tags, the iconographic figures.
- **Annotation** — the human aside in the margin: the taped "✎ A local note."
- **Evidence** — machine-checkable fact: the in-browser "✓ N events re-verified"
  line, source citations, conformance output.
- **History** — the accumulated, unrewritable past: the Sessions list, the
  Record, session seals.
- **Invitation** — beckons a newcomer to act: "the entrance is one file," the
  WELCOME pointers, "Take the Place home."
- **Warning** — caution: OPEN-clause markers, "reality has not voted,"
  emergency-preservation notes.
- **Ritual** — ceremonial marks that confer gravity or status: the "REALITY
  HAS THE FINAL VOTE" stamp, the wax seal, the act of sealing.
- **Furniture** — creates conditions without thinking: the Table, Journal,
  Cabinet, Shelf, Press.

## 4. First census (initial, revisable) — and the collisions that matter

A census is only useful where it exposes a **niche collision**: one species in
two roles, or two species crowding one spot. Three observed:

1. **The taped note occupies two niches — Annotation and History.** The ✎
   "how to enter" note is Annotation (correct, and rare). The Sessions room
   uses the *same* taped-note species as its History list-items — thirteen of
   them. When History repeats, the Annotation species is spent as wallpaper.
   This is the exact, deeper restatement of CAB-014's repetition finding: the
   problem was never repetition; it is that History is wearing Annotation's
   skin. The eventual move (on evidence) is to give History its own species — a
   quiet ledger — and keep the taped note the rare handwritten aside.
2. **Ritual crowds Annotation** at the Constitution room's top: the
   "final vote" stamp overlaps the note beneath it — two loud species in one
   spot.
3. **The observatory window frames Evidence and History as Architecture** —
   open questions and RFC status live inside a structural mass, a mild blur of
   three niches into one container.

## 5. Standing discipline

- Classify every visual object by niche **as evidence arrives** — this census
  is a seed, not a finished map.
- Act only when a collision is shown to **impede a visitor** (Cohort 1 and
  after), never on this lens alone.
- When action is justified, prefer **moving a species into its own niche** over
  adding a new object — the review's rule: make an existing object communicate
  more clearly.
- The goal is not visual consistency. It is that everything **belongs.**
