# Recognition — the Space, and the Laboratory within it

The Reality seat classified this himself: **not an amendment — a recognition
about language and architecture.** It is deposited as such: a recognition
pointing at a property the corpus may already hold, to be adopted *gradually*
"if the corpus agrees," never force-fit.

## 1. The recognition (as authored)

The system began, correctly, as **The Laboratory** — its defining act was
experimentation. Over the recent constitutional work that changed. The system
is now a **place**: an environment containing multiple places with different
constitutional responsibilities — the Journal, the Cabinet, the Shelf, the
Door, the Table, and the Laboratory itself. The whole is therefore no longer
defined by *experimentation* but by **orientation, stewardship, preservation,
discovery, and habitation.**

The important claim is not the name but the **architectural distinction**: the
whole and the part are no longer the same thing. It may be more accurate for
**the Laboratory to become one place within a larger whole**, and for the whole
to be named **the Space** (or possibly **the Habitat**). The Laboratory remains
one important instrument inside that environment — not the environment itself.

## 2. Why this is recognition, not invention (the corpus already reaches for it)

Per the corpus's own posture — *can this be recognized as an existing property?*
— the distinction is largely already present; this act names it at the
whole-system level rather than manufacturing it:

- **RFC-005** already names the Record "**one organ** — episodic memory. The
  Laboratory also has a **body** (code, habitat, …)." Whole ≠ part is stated.
- **RFC-004** already frames the whole as specializing in "**habitation and
  discovery**: a lived" environment — the very definition proposed here.
- **RFC-008** already speaks of interfaces including "**spatial habitats**."
- **Canonical's session 28** (now in the preserved lineage) asked outright
  "whether the Record is the whole Laboratory or one organ" — the same question,
  one axis over.
- The **places already exist** as distinct responsibilities (Journal, Cabinet,
  Shelf, Door, Table, Laboratory); the preserved lineage's tree literally
  carries `table`, `rotating-table.md`, and `releases`.

So the recognition generalizes an existing property; the burden it still bears
is genuineness — that "the Space" names something real and is not a relabeling
for its own sake. That burden is discharged by *use*: future documents that
find the whole/part distinction clarifying will adopt the language; those that
do not, will not. "If the corpus agrees" is exactly this test.

## 3. Design consequence

The interface is not a dashboard and not merely a laboratory: it is a **space
people inhabit**. Different places perform different constitutional
responsibilities; the participant *moves between places*; **orientation emerges
from the environment itself** (cf. the proprioception report and the
Constitutional Map as orientation instruments, RFC-005). The Laboratory is one
instrument within that environment, not the environment.

## 4. Honest residue

This is a Cabinet recognition, not a rename sweep — nothing in the corpus is
renamed by this deposit. Adoption is gradual and voluntary, as its author
intends. Its Record-side anchor (as an observation pointing at existing law,
not a new object) waits, like everything else this session, for the fork's
reconciliation to restore one live tip; recorded honestly here rather than
forced through a side door.
