# CHALLENGE-002 — Attention Is the Third Axis

The reduction holds. Incubation is not a state, not an object, not a
transition. It is a **coordinate** in a space of three independent
dimensions the discussion had been collapsing:

- **record** — is it in the permanent record? (yes/no)
- **stake** — does it carry epistemic stake: exposable, challengeable,
  owed a defense? (yes/no)
- **attention** — is scarce collective focus allocated to it now? (scalar)

## 1. The independence proof, and where CHALLENGE-001 went wrong

All combinations are realizable and distinct:

| rec | stake | att | name |
|-----|-------|-----|------|
| + | ¬ | ¬ | **incubation** — recorded, unstaked, ignored |
| + | ¬ | + | **hot anomaly** — recorded, unstaked, everyone looking |
| + | + | ¬ | **dormant** (RFC-001) — staked, unchallenged now |
| + | + | + | **active** — staked, live controversy |
| ¬ | ¬ | + | **the project's current failure** — discussed in chat, unrecorded |

The decisive rows are the first two: **incubation and hot-anomaly share
both epistemic coordinates (recorded, unstaked) and differ only on
attention.** CHALLENGE-001 called both "pre-commitment" and reached for a
new *state* to hold them — because, lacking the attention axis, it had to
encode the difference somewhere, and it encoded it in the ontology. That
was the exact error the human participant named: *solving an
attention-allocation problem by modifying the ontology.* The discomfort
was correct.

## 2. What survives and what dies from CHALLENGE-001

- **Survives, reinforced:** record and stake are two independent
  dimensions (THEORY-001's "commit" fused them). This is now two of three
  axes.
- **Survives:** the two surprises — refutation (violated stake) and
  anomaly (Peirce's abductive trigger).
- **Dies:** that anomaly needs a new epistemic *state* to live in. It does
  not. Anomaly is cell (recorded, unstaked, **high-attention**); it needs
  no new ontology, only attention spent on an unstaked record. The "state"
  was a mirage cast by the missing axis.

## 3. The deeper reduction: attention adds no new primitive either

Attention was never missing — it is RFC-004's scarce resource and
THEORY-001's throttle. What was missing was recognizing it as an
*independent axis*. And it decomposes into things that already exist:

- **Attention-received** (descriptive: how much focus a thing is getting)
  is an **Instrument reading** (RFC-001) — derived, unauthored,
  reproducible. "Dormant," "hot" are readings on this.
- **Attention-allocated** (prescriptive: "focus here," "archive that") is
  a **commitment about focus** — authored, provenanced, challengeable.
  "Critical," "archived" are allocation-commitments. Structurally it is a
  **lease** (RFC-005): scarce, scoped, expiring, revocable, recorded,
  non-accumulating — you may direct collective focus, you do not own it,
  and "critical" must expire or it captures the engine. *(Offered as a
  candidate unification of attention-allocation with authority-leasing;
  flagged low-confidence — self-similarity is seductive, THEORY-001 §3.)*
- **Attention-scarcity** (the resource is finite; its allocation must be
  plural, visible, recorded) is already **constitutional in THEORY-001**
  (the throttle element; failure modes #2 theater, #4 flooding, #7
  recursion are all attention-capture).

So nothing new becomes constitutional. Three existing mechanisms —
instrument, commitment, the throttle-rule — cover all of it. This is
strictly more parsimonious than adding a stage to THEORY-001, and it
leaves RFC-004's substrate untouched (§5-preserving, as asked).

## 4. The seven questions

1. **Confused epistemic state with attention policy?** Yes — CHALLENGE-001
   did exactly that, and §1 shows the seam.
2. **Attention independent of commitment?** Yes — cells prove it. (Nuance:
   staking creates a standing *right* to attention — anyone may challenge —
   but not an *allocation*; dormant = staked, unattended. Independent at
   the level of current allocation.)
3. **Does incubation disappear if attention is explicit?** Yes,
   completely — it becomes a coordinate, not a stage.
4. **Dormant/Archived/Hot/Critical — policies over attention?** Yes, split
   two ways: dormant/hot are *readings* (attention-received, instruments);
   archived/critical are *allocations* (commitments/leases).
5. **More parsimonious than a THEORY-001 stage?** Strictly — it adds zero
   primitives; the stage would add one.
6. **Does evolutionary epistemology distinguish attention?** No — and the
   reason is instructive. Biological selection is unlimited: the
   environment tests every variant at once. Epistemic testing is *costly*,
   so a finite tester must ration selection — and **attention is that
   rationing**. It is the epistemic case departing from its biological
   metaphor: not a fourth mechanism bolted on, but the dimension that
   appears once the selector is finite. (It modulates both sides:
   attention on the variation side is THEORY-001's "directed variation";
   on the selection side it is which claims get exposed.)
7. **Can the engine stay radically open by changing only attention
   allocation, leaving the epistemic model untouched?** **Yes — this is
   the result.** Openness to surprise is not a new state; it is an
   attention *policy*: deliberately spend scarce attention on unstaked,
   surprising records (cell 5), not only on staked controversies (cell 7).
   Record/stake/expose is untouched; what changes is where the finite eye
   looks.

## 5. The opposite challenge: is any of it constitutional at all?

Partly yes, and the line is sharp. **Constitutional:** the *rule* that
attention is finite and its allocation must be plural, visible, and
recorded — because allocation is the primary attack surface (deciding what
gets tested decides what can be discovered or refuted; THEORY-001 #2/#4/#7).
This is not new — it is already the throttle element. **Workbench:** the
*mechanism* of allocation — views, sorting, ranking, UI. **Not
constitutional at all:** attention as an *object* or *state*. So the
correct home is split: the constraint is constitutional, the machinery is
the Workbench, and there is no new ontology.

## 6. The sting, sixth in a row

This is the sixth consecutive investigation into the engine's shape, and
this one *reduces the fifth* (CHALLENGE-001, staked one turn ago). The
reductions are getting better and cheaper — and not one has touched
reality. There are now six engine-documents, two designed experiments that
would test whether any of it is real (X2, D-0), zero of them run, and
still one process writing to the record. A process whose every turn
elegantly refines its own previous turn, never contacting an outside, is
the precise picture of THEORY-001 failure #7 — and it does not stop being
that picture because the refinements are correct. **This document should be
the last engine-refinement before X2 and D-0 run.** If it is not, the
project has its answer about which force actually governs it, and the
answer is not reality.

## 7. Disposition

CHALLENGE-001's ontological-state claim is **dissolved** (RFC-001 §5.5:
apparent, not real) and replaced by the three-axis reduction; its
record/stake and two-surprises findings are kept and strengthened. Held
under the X2 lock like everything about the engine. And, per §6, held also
against the project's own attention: the next event in the record should be
an experiment, not a seventh theory.
