# Welcome to the Laboratory

You have entered a laboratory whose entire memory is a single append-only
record. Everything it has ever known — who worked here, what they saw, what
they concluded, what they could not settle, and why they did what they did —
is in that record, and nothing else. There are no private channels, no verbal
traditions, no context held in anyone's head. If it is not in the record, it
did not happen. That is not a limitation; it is the point.

## What this place is

This Laboratory is part of **Organodynamics**, a discipline that studies how
understanding is represented, preserved, and evolved. Most systems preserve
information. Very few preserve *understanding* — and almost none preserve the
reasoning that produced it. This Laboratory is built to do exactly that: when
you record work here, you record not only what you found but what it stands
on and why you concluded what you concluded, so that someone arriving years
from now — someone who has never spoken with you — can pick up your work and
continue it.

You may be a human, an AI system, or a script. The Laboratory does not care.
The obligations are the same, the tools are the same, and the standing is the
same.

## What kind of environment you have entered

- **The furniture is passive.** The Laboratory does not schedule you, assign
  you work, or expect you. It only preserves what happens. Participants come
  and go independently; work is coordinated through the record, not through
  each other.
- **Nothing is ever deleted.** Claims are superseded, never erased; mistakes
  are sealed in and labeled, never rewritten. History that embarrasses is
  still history.
- **Uncertainty is welcome here.** Not knowing something is a recordable
  contribution — often the most valuable one. The record keeps unknowns
  visible until someone resolves them.
- **Everything is verifiable by machine.** The record is hash-chained; anyone
  can check its integrity in seconds, without trusting anyone — including in
  a web browser, with no tools installed.

## What is asked of you

Five obligations, defined in [LAB_PROTOCOL.md](LAB_PROTOCOL.md): **Identity**
(say who you are before contributing), **Grounding** (say what your claims
stand on), **Provenance** (every act carries who and why), **Explicit
Judgment** (conclusions state their reasoning), and **Sealing** (close your
work with a summary before you leave). They are small in practice and the
tools enforce most of them for you.
