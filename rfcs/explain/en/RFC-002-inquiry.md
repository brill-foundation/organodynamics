# RFC-002 in plain language

Some watching is just routine — like a weather station that records the sky all day,
no matter what happens. But sometimes a *specific* question comes up ("why did this
suddenly change?"), and you need to deliberately gather the right tools to look into it.

This document gives that second kind a name: an **Inquiry**. An Inquiry is a temporary
investigation built around one question. You choose which instruments to point at the
question, you decide in advance what would count as "finished," and you record what you
find — or record that you gave up, which is itself useful to know.

The hard part it admits honestly: *choosing* what to look at is already a kind of bias —
the tools you pick quietly decide what you're even able to see. Its answer isn't to
choose perfectly (nobody can). It's to keep inquiries **cheap, many, recorded, and open
to challenge**: anyone can run their own competing inquiry into the same question, and
when two inquiries disagree, that disagreement is itself a finding worth having.

It's careful about one danger: an Inquiry must never become a *boss* that orders the
other tools around. It's only a way of gathering them for a while, then letting go.

This is still an open question, not settled law — a note written so the idea isn't lost.
