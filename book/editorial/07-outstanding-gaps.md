# Editorial Deliverable 10 — Outstanding Gaps

*What the book does not yet have, stated honestly. A gap is not a failure of recovery; it is
a recovered fact about the manuscript's real state of completion. The discipline treats a
dormant door as an observation, not a deficiency — this register does the same.*

Each gap records: what is missing, why it is missing (was it ever written?), what stands in
its place now, and what would close it. Gaps are ordered roughly by how much they limit the
reader.

---

## G-1 — The book's prose was never written as prose
- **Missing:** original, continuous chapter prose. The nine `drafts/*.md` files are 0 bytes.
- **Why:** the author committed the book's *skeleton* (titled chapter order) and its *ideas*
  (the RFCs), but never the connecting essays.
- **Stands in its place:** a manuscript woven from recovered RFC text, fitted to the
  skeleton, with editorial bridges marked `[ed.]`.
- **Closes when:** an author writes the connecting essays *on top of* the recovered canon —
  additively, never overwriting the RFC-sourced doctrine.
- **Severity:** structural, but addressed. This is the reconstruction's central fact, not a
  defect in it.

## G-3 — The invitational voice of "Come" is a frame, not a text
- **Missing:** the lyrical, second-person invitation the chapter title promises.
- **Why:** no such prose exists in the Record. The *content* of the invitation is canonical
  (RFC-002: Inquiries are "cheap, plural, recorded, contestable"; "anyone may open" one; the
  default is no restriction), but its *lyrical expression* was never written.
- **Stands in its place:** Chapter 3 recovers the invitation's substance and marks the
  missing lyric as pencil (Stage I).
- **Closes when:** an author writes the invitation essay, drawing only on the canonical
  openness already placed there.
- **Severity:** moderate; the chapter is honest about being a frame.

## G-5 — "Personal Worlds" as a lived idea is under-sourced
- **Missing:** the fuller idea the title implies — that each researcher inhabits a personal
  world of authored, confidence-bearing representations.
- **Why:** the canon supports the *pieces* (representations are authored; confidence is
  declared; the specimen is the representation, never the author; no per-author metrics) but
  never assembles them into a "personal worlds" thesis.
- **Stands in its place:** Chapter 5 assembles the canonical pieces and flags the synthesis
  as investigation-stage (✎▸), not settled doctrine.
- **Closes when:** the synthesis is either written up and *promoted* into a representation
  (opening a door, per RFC-001 §5.4) or found already latent in a source not yet in the Record.

## G-7 — "The Sun" as an image is pencil; only its claim is ink
- **Missing:** the poetic development of Reality-as-Sun.
- **Why:** the *claim* is hard canon (Reality is the one unproduced anchor, "the only element
  of the diagram nothing else produces", RFC-001 §7). The *image* — the Sun — is the editor's
  reading of the title `07-the-sun.md`, not a figure the author drew in words.
- **Stands in its place:** Chapter 7 states the canonical claim in ink and renders the Sun
  image explicitly as a pencil frame (FIG-7.1), performing the gap rather than hiding it.
- **Closes when:** an author writes the image out, or the interlock is corrected (see G-2).
- **Severity:** low; clearly marked.

## G-2 — The chapter interlock is well-supported but not certified by the author
- **Missing:** any recovered statement from the author confirming that this skeleton was
  meant for *this* content in *this* mapping.
- **Why:** the mapping is inferred from shared vocabulary (decisively, *door* = representation).
  It is strong, but it is an inference.
- **Stands in its place:** the Canonical Source Map's interlock table, offered as a proposal
  resolvable by challenge (Editorial Report §7).
- **Closes when:** the author confirms, adjusts, or refutes the mapping. Any of these is a
  welcome move; refutation turns this reconstruction into a dated waypoint.

## G-4 — No specifications, research notes, or conversations exist to draw on
- **Missing:** four of the eight Canonical-Priority tiers (Specifications, Knowledge Base,
  Research Notes, Conversations) have **no artifacts** in the repository.
- **Why:** the project is early; `docs/`, `references/`, `research/` are empty scaffolding.
- **Stands in its place:** nothing is claimed from these tiers. The book rests entirely on
  the RFC and documentation tiers.
- **Closes when:** those directories fill. The Living Book process (Deliverable 11) says how
  to fold them in without erasing present understanding.

## G-6 — Implementation is undecided by design
- **Missing:** schemas, scripts, directory layout, the maturity vocabulary, reading cadence,
  the initial Observatory roster.
- **Why:** RFC-001 §8 and RFC-002 §6 deliberately decline to decide these. "Those follow
  understanding; this RFC is the understanding."
- **Stands in its place:** Chapter 8 records these as open on purpose; the book does not
  invent an implementation.
- **Closes when:** future RFCs decide them. Each decision is a new door and a new chapter
  candidate.

## G-8 — The richer illustrations (atlas plates) are specified, not produced
- **Missing:** FIG-4.1-atlas, FIG-7.1-plate, FIG-0.1-mark — the full scientific-atlas
  renderings.
- **Why:** they require a raster/vector tool and craft; shipping-now figures cover the
  meaning in Mermaid/text.
- **Stands in its place:** every meaning is already conveyed by a ship-now figure; the plates
  add polish, not doctrine.
- **Closes when:** an illustrator produces them into `assets/diagrams/` and `assets/logos/`
  against the fixed frames in the Illustration Plan.
