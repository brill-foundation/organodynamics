# Editorial Deliverable 5 — Editorial Report

*The reconstruction's reasoning, decisions, and limits, in one place.*

---

## 1. Mandate

Recover *The Brill Blueprint* — the book that already exists, scattered across the
repository — and publish it as one continuously readable manuscript, with its history
preserved and its evolution expressed visually. Not to write a better book; to recover
the book that is there.

## 2. What was there

Two things, and the space between them:

1. **A canon** — RFC-001 (*The Instrument*, three revisions) and RFC-002 (*Inquiry*).
   Dense, finished, load-bearing prose in the author's voice. This is where the book's
   *ideas* live.
2. **A skeleton** — nine empty, deliberately titled chapter files in `drafts/`. This is
   where the book's *shape* lives: Door, Vision, Organodynamics, Come, Reality Layers,
   Personal Worlds, Hospitality, The Sun, Open Questions.

The space between them is the reconstruction problem. The prose the chapter titles seem to
promise — warm, invitational, almost liturgical ("Come", "Hospitality", "The Sun") — was
never written. The prose that *was* written is cool and exact. The recovery is the act of
recognizing that these are two halves of one design, joined by a shared vocabulary in which
a *representation* is a *door*, *Reality* is the unmoved anchor, and *disagreement is
information*.

## 3. Governing decisions

**D-1 — Recover, do not invent.** Where canonical text exists, it is reproduced or closely
paraphrased with provenance. Where a chapter title outruns its evidence, the manuscript
does *not* manufacture doctrine to fill the gap. It builds the chapter from the nearest
canonical vocabulary, states plainly what is recovered and what is a frame, and logs the
shortfall in Outstanding Gaps.

**D-2 — Preserve the evolution of thought.** RFC-001's three revisions record the
discipline changing its mind twice (the Observatory; Reality above the Record) and
deepening once (disagreement is information). These are recovered as a first-class part of
the book — Appendix C and per-chapter historical sidebars — not smoothed away. The book
documents its own creation, as the mandate requires.

**D-3 — Latest revision is canonical; earlier belief is a dated waypoint.** Applied to all
five conflicts (see Canonical Source Map §2). Nothing is discarded; superseded claims are
kept and dated.

**D-4 — The repository remains the source of truth.** The manuscript is explicitly a
*derived* artifact. The RFCs stay in `/rfcs`; the book cites and re-derives. Any reader can
audit the book against the canon.

**D-5 — Visual maturity tracks evidentiary maturity, honestly.** The pencil→ink metamorphosis
is not decoration applied by chapter number. A passage is rendered in "ink" when it rests on
settled canonical text and in "pencil" when it is a frame awaiting prose. This makes the
Chrysalis Principle *true* rather than merely stylistic: the book looks as certain as it
actually is, section by section. See the Visual Language Guide.

**D-6 — Single voice.** Terminology was fixed (Canonical Source Map §3) so chapters woven
from different RFC sections read as one author. No idea was restyled for style's sake (per
the Editorial Policy); rewriting was confined to merging duplicates, bridging, and
resolving conflicts.

## 4. How each chapter was built

- **Densely recovered (▣/▮):** 2 Organodynamics, 4 Reality Layers, 6 Hospitality, 8 Open
  Questions, and the doctrinal spine of 0 The Door, 1 Vision, 5 Personal Worlds, 7 The Sun.
  These rest on explicit RFC text; the editor's hand is limited to ordering and bridging.
- **Frame-led (✎▸/✎):** 3 Come and the *essayistic* register of 0, 5, and 7. Here the title
  promises a mode of address the canon never wrote out. The chapters recover the *content*
  the title implies (the invitation is real: "anyone may open an Inquiry"; the "personal
  world" is real: representations are authored and confidence-bearing) and mark the
  missing lyrical prose as a gap.

## 5. What the reconstruction deliberately did **not** do

- It did not write the missing lyrical essays ("Come", "The Sun" as standalone poems).
  Those would be invention. Their frames are set and their canonical content placed; the
  prose is left for a future author, logged as gaps G-3, G-7.
- It did not resolve any open question. Q-a … Q-i and RFC-002's Q-a … Q-d remain open, as
  the canon leaves them.
- It did not alter, correct, or "finish" the RFCs. They are the source of truth and are
  reproduced verbatim in the appendices.
- It did not invent visual assets as finished art. The illustration *plan* specifies every
  figure; the diagrams that ship are the ones derivable directly from canonical text.

## 6. Confidence

Following the discipline's own convention, this reconstruction declares its confidence:

- **Settled:** the inventory, the fragment catalogue, the provenance of every doctrinal
  paragraph, the five conflict resolutions, and the verbatim appendices. These are facts of
  the Record.
- **Well-supported:** the chapter interlock — the claim that the skeleton and the RFCs were
  built for each other. The *door* hinge (a representation is a door) is explicit canon; the
  rest of the mapping is strongly implied by shared vocabulary.
- **Speculative (flagged):** the specific lyrical intent behind "Come", "Personal Worlds",
  and "The Sun". The titles are recovered; the essays they name are not. The manuscript
  treats these as frames, not findings.

## 7. Disposition

This report, like the RFCs it serves, is resolved by challenge and revision. If the
chapter interlock is wrong — if the skeleton was meant for a different book — the correction
is a welcome one, and this reconstruction becomes a dated waypoint, exactly as the
discipline prescribes for its own superseded beliefs.
