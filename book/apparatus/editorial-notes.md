# Editorial Notes

*Reader-facing notes on the conventions, judgment calls, and boundaries of this edition. For the
full reasoning, see the [Editorial Report](../editorial/04-editorial-report.md); for the complete
apparatus, the [`book/editorial/`](../editorial/) dossier.*

---

## On what "recovered" means here

This manuscript is a **derived artifact**. It does not add doctrine to Organodynamics; it
*arranges* the doctrine the repository already holds into a continuous read. The test applied to
every doctrinal sentence was: *can this be traced to RFC-001, RFC-002, or the README?* If yes, it
appears with its source. If no, it is either an editorial bridge (marked `[ed.]`, asserting
nothing) or it does not appear at all.

## On the `[ed.]` mark

Sentences that exist only to join two recovered passages into readable prose are editorial
connective tissue. They carry no doctrine and are marked `[ed.]` in the provenance footers. If
you are auditing the book against the canon, `[ed.]` sentences are the ones you can ignore for
doctrinal purposes — they are the mortar, not the stones.

## On the chapter interlock

The single largest editorial judgment is the claim that the empty chapter skeleton and the RFCs
were *made for each other* — that "The Door" means the representation-as-door, "Reality Layers"
means RFC-001 §7's hierarchy, "The Sun" means Reality-as-anchor, and so on. This is
**well-supported but not certified by the author** (the *door* = representation hinge is explicit
canon; the rest is strong inference from shared vocabulary). It is offered as a challengeable
proposal. If the author intended a different mapping, this edition becomes a dated waypoint —
exactly what the discipline prescribes for its own superseded beliefs. See
[Gap G-2](../editorial/07-outstanding-gaps.md).

## On chapters that are frames

Three chapters carry titles whose lyrical prose was never written: **Come**, **Personal
Worlds**, and **The Sun**. For each, the book recovers the *canonical substance* the title
implies and leaves the missing lyric visibly in pencil (`✎`), rather than inventing warmth to
fill the frame. These are honest frames, not finished essays; see Gaps
[G-3, G-5, G-7](../editorial/07-outstanding-gaps.md).

## On conflicts and the historical layer

The discipline changed its mind twice and deepened a belief once. Every such change is preserved,
not smoothed: the superseded claim is kept, dated, and cross-linked to what replaced it, in
**historical sidebars** and in [Appendix C](../appendices/C-evolution-of-rfc-001.md). The rule
was always *latest canon wins; earlier belief is kept as a waypoint.* The five resolved conflicts
are tabulated in the [Canonical Source Map §2](../editorial/02-canonical-source-map.md).

## On terminology

To make chapters woven from different RFC sections read in one voice, spellings and senses were
fixed (Canonical Source Map §3) — *door*, *Instrument*, *the Observatory*, *the Goodhart line*,
and the rest. No idea was restyled merely for style; rewriting was confined to merging
duplicates, bridging, and resolving conflicts, per the mandate's editorial policy.

## On the visual language

The pencil→ink metamorphosis is **semantic, not decorative**. A badge is set by how settled the
underlying evidence is, not by where the chapter falls. This is why an early chapter can carry
ink and a late one pencil. The convention set is the [Visual Language
Guide](../editorial/06-visual-language-guide.md).

## On what was not done

This edition did **not**: write the missing lyrical essays; resolve any open question; alter,
correct, or "finish" the RFCs; or produce the atlas-grade illustration plates (their frames are
specified for a future illustrator). Each of these is logged as a gap, visible in the manuscript
at the point it bites.

## On keeping the book alive

The manuscript is a **Living Book**. When new canonical knowledge lands, follow the update loop
in [Future Recommendations](../editorial/08-future-recommendations.md): locate affected chapters,
diff the doctrine, re-badge maturity, update the apparatus, and record the update as an event in
the Record — never erasing what the book used to know.
