# How to Read This Book

*The Brill Blueprint behaves like a knowledge system, not just a sequence of pages. A few
conventions let you read it at whatever depth you need and always know where you are.*

## Three reading depths

Every chapter supports three depths. Choose per chapter; you can mix them.

1. **Blueprint depth.** Each chapter opens with a **Blueprint box** — three to five lines that
   state the whole chapter in précis. Read only the Blueprint boxes, front to back, and you
   have read the book's skeleton in a few minutes.
2. **Reading depth.** The chapter prose itself. This is the book as a continuous read.
3. **Canon depth.** The `▣` clauses, the historical sidebars, the provenance footers, and the
   appendix cross-links — for readers who want to audit the manuscript against the RFCs.

## The maturity badge — how sure is this?

Every chapter, section, and figure carries one **maturity badge**, telling you how settled the
underlying idea is. The badges are applied by *evidence*, not by chapter number.

| Badge | Stage | The page is saying |
|-------|-------|--------------------|
| `✎` | Discovery (pencil) | "We are discovering." A frame, a sketch, a question. |
| `✎▸` | Investigation (pencil + ink) | "We believe this, but we are still testing." |
| `▮` | Validation (ink) | "This has survived testing." |
| `▣` | Canonical (specification) | "This representation is currently canonical." |

Each chapter also opens with a **maturity meter** — a small bar showing the chapter's dominant
stage at a glance:

```
Maturity  ✎──✎▸──▮──▣     this chapter reaches: ▣ Canonical
          ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓
```

Across the book the ink accumulates — early chapters carry more pencil, later ones more ink —
because certainty accumulates. But locally, honesty wins: a settled claim in an early chapter
is inked, and an unwritten essay in a late chapter stays pencil. This is the **Chrysalis
Principle**: the book performs the transformation it describes, maturing in view from notebook
toward specification.

## Provenance footers — where did this come from?

Every chapter ends with a **Provenance** block listing the exact sources of its claims:

- `R1 §5.4` — RFC-001, section 5.4 (canonical revision 3 unless a revision is named);
- `R1@rev2 §6` — a *superseded* passage, cited from an earlier revision;
- `R2 §3` — RFC-002, section 3;
- `README`, `SKEL:00-door`, `EVO-1/2` — documentation, a skeleton frame, a revision diff;
- `[ed.]` — an editorial bridge, asserting no doctrine.

The book is a lens; the repository is the sky. Anything marked with a source can be checked
against the verbatim RFCs in the appendices.

## Historical sidebars — what changed its mind?

Blockquoted **historical sidebars** mark the places where the discipline changed or deepened a
belief. They preserve *what was first believed* alongside *what became canonical*. The full
story is [Appendix C · The Evolution of RFC-001](../appendices/C-evolution-of-rfc-001.md).

## Navigating

- Each chapter footer has **← previous / → next** links and a list of key **concepts** linking
  to the [Glossary](../apparatus/glossary.md).
- The [Table of Contents](../SUMMARY.md) carries a **knowledge map** showing how the chapters
  and concepts connect.
- The [Illustration Index](../apparatus/illustration-index.md) lists every figure by ID.

## A suggested first path

If you read nothing else: the nine **Blueprint boxes**, then [Chapter 4 · Reality
Layers](../manuscript/04-reality-layers.md) (the central spread) and [Chapter 7 · The
Sun](../manuscript/07-the-sun.md) (the anchor) in full. That is the discipline's spine and its
still point, in about twenty minutes.
