> **Blueprint.** The project wrote down *how it develops itself* — RFC-000, the Development
> Manifest — and then reviewed it as ruthlessly as any other representation. The review
> found "a strong statement of values and a weak constitution," and extracted from it a
> **seven-invariant core**. This chapter is the manifest and its demolition: the moment the
> discipline turned its own machinery on its own foundation.

The Recovery Edition ended with a discipline that could observe representations (the
Instrument, the Observatory) but had no account of how it should *govern its own
development*. Chapter 9 is where the project supplies one — and immediately submits it to
challenge. The register changes here: we have left the philosophical notebook and arrived at
the engineer's desk, where drafts are pinned up to be corrected.

---

### ▣ Technical

**RFC-000 — the Development Manifest** declares that Organodynamics is not merely a software
project, a research project, or a book, but *an evolving architecture for improving human
understanding*, and that it has **three inseparable layers**:

| Layer | Name | Output |
|-------|------|--------|
| I — Philosophy | *The Brill Blueprint* | books, essays (audience: humans) |
| II — Architecture | Organodynamics | RFCs, Constitution, reference models |
| III — Implementation | Brill Center | code, APIs, protocols |

> 🟢 insight — *this book is Layer I.* RFC-000 names the manuscript you are reading as the
> project's philosophy layer. The Living Edition is that layer catching up to the other two.

The manifest sets out principles that still stand: *representation before authority* (people
do not compete, representations do); *reality before representation* (evidence outranks any
representation); *every disagreement is architectural* (a disagreement is evidence something
is missing — context, scale, definitions); *failure is preserved*; *AI systems are
contributors, not authorities*; and *the living constitution* — "nothing is immutable,
including this principle."

Then the **constitutional review of RFC-000** did what the discipline demands. Its verdict:

> 🔴 tension — "a strong statement of epistemic values and a **weak constitution**." It
> confused three artifacts — a constitution (entrenched invariants + amendment rule), bylaws
> (revisable process), and a 2026 roadmap (the three named layers). It had **no decision
> rule**, no membership model, no precedence rule, no adversarial assumption, no right to
> exit — and a self-undermining mutability clause (if the mutability rule is itself mutable,
> the first amendment can seize the amendment mechanism).

The review's constructive core is a **seven-clause constitution**, extracted and
strengthened — the spine the rest of the architecture hangs from:

1. **Supremacy of Reality** — evidence outranks any representation, process, or value.
2. **The Record** — history preserved append-only, tombstones not erasures; testimony about
   the world, constitutive only of the discipline's own acts.
3. **Plurality** — no total vantage point at any scale; disagreement juxtaposed, never
   averaged; disputes may terminate as *held*.
4. **Persons are not specimens** — representations compete; archives never rank people.
5. **Provenance** — every artifact versioned, inspectable, attributable, challengeable;
   independence established by provenance, not assertion.
6. **Ratification and stewardship** — nothing enters canon without recorded challenge and an
   explicit, named decision rule; integration is an office with selection, term, removal.
7. **Exit** — the Record is portable and forkable; the right to fork is the check of last
   resort.

Everything else is bylaws. (Full text: [`reviews/RFC-000-constitutional-review.md`](../../../reviews/RFC-000-constitutional-review.md);
[`rfcs/RFC-000-development-manifest.md`](../../../rfcs/RFC-000-development-manifest.md).)

> ⌫ erased — ~~"nothing inside this document is immutable; every principle may evolve,
> including this principle"~~ → superseded by **tiered rigidity**: a few entrenched
> invariants with an expensive amendment path, everything else ordinary bylaws. Kept as
> tombstone; the manifest's uniform-mutability clause was the review's clearest single
> correction.

### ◇ Human

Imagine writing the rules for your own project, publishing them, and then inviting the
sharpest critic you can find to prove they would collapse under pressure — and *thanking*
them when they succeed. That is what happened here, and it is the emotional centre of the
whole discipline. The manifest was not defended; it was *studied as a phenomenon.* Its
author's values survived; its author's authority did not — because the review noticed that
where a constitution forgets to say who decides, the founder decides by default, and a
shadow monarchy is worse than a declared one, since a declared one can be checked.

The seven invariants matter to a person because they are the smallest set of promises that
make a knowledge commons safe to join: *reality can always overrule us; the past cannot be
rewritten; no one voice is the whole truth; you will never be scored as a person; everything
is attributable; nothing becomes law in secret; and you can always leave and take the record
with you.* A newcomer can read those seven and know what kind of place this is.

### ⏱ Chronicle

RFC-000 was circulated for review (`c87ebef3`), reviewed adversarially (`6ed9b11a`), and much
later imported verbatim into the repository (`d36cce4e`) — before that, the subject of the
project's first Meta Review existed only in conversation. The review's exposure of the
missing decision rule set off the whole cascade of Act II. Full narrative:
[Chronicle · Act II — The Manifest Days](../chronicle/02-the-manifest-days.md).

> 🔴 tension — the ratification debt the review exposed (Q-f) is **still open**. Nothing in
> the project has been ratified under a stated rule; acceptance happens by *continuation*
> (READING-001). This chapter is blue ink, not black, for exactly that reason: the manifest
> survived challenge, but neither it nor its seven-invariant successor has been *ratified* —
> because the rule for ratifying does not yet exist. See [Ch. 14](14-the-open-constitution.md).
